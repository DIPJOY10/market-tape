Read the numbers with these caveats

A low P/E on peak cyclical earnings is not cheap

Memory and storage (MU, SNDK, WDC, STX) and refiners and tankers (DINO, FRO, TNK) screen at 5–13× forward earnings after revenue growth of 20–175%. Micron's latest quarter grew revenue 346% year over year. These multiples are the market telling you those earnings are not the run rate.

Alphabet's headline P/E is flattered by about $93B of non-operating gains

Trailing net income is $244.2B against operating income of $151.6B. On operating earnings the multiple is closer to 32× than the 17× that screens. It also spent $132.4B of capex to produce $53.3B of free cash flow.

The AI capex bill is starting to land in earnings

Meta grew revenue +27.7% but diluted EPS fell −3.9% as $89.3B of capex flows into depreciation. Oracle carries −$28.7B of free cash flow and 2.53× debt-to-equity against a $664B backlog. Expect this pattern to widen across hyperscalers over the next several quarters.

Price targets are the sell side's opinion, not a forecast

Consensus targets in this table imply 20–80% upside on dozens of names. In a tape where the median stock is down in the past month, that usually means targets are stale rather than that returns are free. Small-cap targets with fewer than eight analysts are especially thin.

Scoring. Value, quality and growth are percentile ranks — value within sector, quality and growth across the whole universe. Short-term blends 1- and 3-month momentum, analyst sentiment and RSI position; long-term blends quality, growth, valuation and leverage. These are descriptive rankings of published numbers, not forecasts.

This is not investment advice. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell any security. "Undervalued" means cheap against peers and its own fundamentals on these metrics — a different claim from "will go up".
